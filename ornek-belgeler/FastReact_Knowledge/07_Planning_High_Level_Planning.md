## High Level Planning

The High Level planning board allows the Planner to plan their orders into groups such as Factories or Subcontractors. It is infinite capacity planning in that it will allow these groups to become overloaded, but will always show the state of loading so that such overload, or under loads, are not overlooked. This approach is in contrast with the Low Level Planning which strictly enforces finite capacity planning.

Orders are represented by coloured strips on a planning board where the colour represents the status or that order (e.g. Late, Materials problems) and the length represents the time in production. The High Level planning board also supports cascading where multiple stages in the production process may be independently planned.

The High Level planning board can be operated manually or automatically using the optional APS functionality and it can link in with multiple Low Level plans. In this way the task of planning may be split so that a central authority could plan orders by allocating them to a factory or subcontractor. Each factory or subcontractor could then use a Low Level planning board to plan in detail, to a specific production line, with build up allowances and so on. Any changes that are made at Low Level are reflected on the High Level plan. See also Distributed Planning.

The High level planning board has the following components:

- Load list
- Grid Style Load List
- Planning board
- Drop Down Planning Board
- Traditional Planning Board
- Moving Orders
- Loading charts
- Tool bar
- Find function
- High Level Right Click Functionality
